Card colour · fishbowl treatment

Fourteen.

Your six at the top, eight more below. Each hue holds a band before it transitions, so the three zones stay distinguishable rather than dissolving into one another.

More to consider

Eight further triads on the same recipe, filling the parts of the wheel your six leave empty — earth, olive, green, teal, blue, red, and one neutral.

The recipe

Eight stops instead of five. The duplicated stops are what create the territory — each hue sits flat for a band before the mix begins, so you read three zones rather than one continuous wash.

background:
  radial-gradient(130% 110% at 88% 98%, rgba(3,3,6,.52), transparent 62%),
  linear-gradient(146deg,
    var(--c1)                                       0%,
    var(--c1)                                      11%,
    color-mix(in oklab, var(--c1) 52%, var(--c2))   33%,
    var(--c2)                                      51%,
    var(--c2)                                      59%,
    color-mix(in oklab, var(--c2) 46%, var(--c3))   79%,
    var(--c3)                                      95%,
    var(--c3)                                     100%);

The radial on top shades the bottom-right corner, which deepens the card without dulling the hues — darkening the hexes alone flattens them.

Mixes are still computed in oklab. sRGB loses lightness through the middle of a blend and that dip is what read as a grey band.
