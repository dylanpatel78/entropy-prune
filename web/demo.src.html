    Eigenvalue spectrum of S

    Eigenvalues are the squared singular values of E, sorted largest first, and they always sum to n because the diagonal of S is exactly 1. Dividing them by n gives a probability distribution for free — that is what Shannon entropy consumes. A tall first bar with a long flat tail means the context says one thing repeatedly.

    Effective rank is exp(H) where H = −Σ pᵢ log pᵢ and p = λ/n. It answers “how many genuinely distinct things are in this context?” — a continuous count, not an integer. Twenty identical chunks score 1.0; twenty unrelated chunks score 20.0. Eigenvalues are computed with a cyclic Jacobi rotation solver; the similarity matrix was precomputed once from the real encoder, and every subset you select is a principal submatrix of it.
